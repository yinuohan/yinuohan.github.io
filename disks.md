## Disk databases

Astrophysical objects are often spheres or disks, and some of the more close-to-human-scale disks are those that form (protoplanetary disks) or are found in (debris disks) planetary systems. Have some fun exploring these disks below. Look how much progress we've made. 

These databases are inspired by Karl Stapelfeldt's [Catalog of Circumstellar Disks](https://www.circumstellardisks.org), Nicole Pawellek's [database of resolved debris disks](https://www.physik.uni-jena.de/21956/catalog-of-resolved-debris-disks) and the [Catalogue of Exoplanets](https://exoplanet.eu/catalog/). They were made with the help of Claude, which I recently gained access to thanks to Caltech. They are not comprehensive, though I tried to be as much as possible with the limited time I had. 

If you're doing some serious science with these databases, please be very careful. It might be a good idea to check with me where things come from, as I cannot guarantee that they are accurate. 

### [Debris disk explorer](https://yinuohan.github.io/debris-explorer/)

All the resolved debris disks. Come check out some cousins of our Kuiper belt. 


### [YSOs and protoplanetary disk explorer](https://yinuohan.github.io/yso-explorer/)

All the resolved planet-forming disks. These will eventually become the ones above plus some planets, if we're thinking along the right track. 

What planets and debris disks will they form? Only time will tell--it takes a few million years for them to make an actual planetary system. That's about an order of magnitude longer than the time since the emergence of early modern humans, but more than an order magnitude faster than the cycle of plate tectonics. Perhaps it's comforting to know that future humans (or their AI agents) might be able to test all our predictions for sure, just in time before the map of the world becomes unrecognisable, assuming we still live on this particular planet, and other caveats relating to sea levels etc.
